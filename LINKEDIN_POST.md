## Full post version

I’d like to share **เพื่อนพิง PuengPing**, a prototype web application I built to make case tracking and service coordination for people experiencing homelessness more structured and easier to act on.

The project started from a simple question:

If each case is not only about “shelter,” but also connected to health, documents and rights, welfare access, mental wellbeing, work skills, and follow-up across multiple organizations, how can we help staff see the full picture and make better-informed decisions?

PuengPing is designed as a shared workspace for field teams and service coordinators. The main features include:

- An overview dashboard for urgent cases, service needs, and recent referrals
- Search and filtering by area, need type, risk level, shelter status, and follow-up status
- Case profiles that bring together health, shelter, rights, skills, constraints, and referral history
- Service-needs grouping to support more targeted coordination
- A Thai-language AI assistant that helps search, summarize, and identify skills that may match employment opportunities

One design principle I cared about throughout the project was that AI should support staff, not replace human judgment. The AI assistant is positioned as a search and summarization tool. Its output still needs human review, especially when the information involves health, safety, welfare rights, or employment suitability.

The prototype was built with **Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Recharts, and Typhoon AI**. It currently uses mock data only, but building it helped me think more deeply about multi-role workflows, responsible AI design, and how complex case data can be made easier to understand without reducing people to a single problem.

For me, good technology should not only make work faster. It should help people see needs more clearly, coordinate support more responsibly, and keep human dignity at the center of the system.

**PuengPing — because effective support starts with seeing the whole person, not just one problem.**

I’m open to feedback, suggestions, and ideas for improving the concept.
